Instructional Scope

📚 Curriculum Map

See the prefixes, roots, suffixes, and practice vocabulary currently included in First Volo Morphology. Word parts are introduced progressively and then revisited in increasingly varied and complex words.

Scope and sequence with words in context

One scope and sequence for both online formats. A Migration Route shows where a word part is introduced. Open a Route and a meaning group to see the parts, what they mean or do, and an example in a sentence. Use the learner’s needs to choose a starting group. Earlier parts can return in new words and in set review.

Independent online learning In Learn, explore word parts in meaning groups, see examples, and sort and explain where a comparison fits. Practice the same parts in activities such as Word Part and Figure It Out. In Route 3, Figure It Out asks the learner to check their first answer against a sentence clue.
Teacher-guided online sessions In the Next Session Guide, use saved practice or choose a part and activity from the same Routes. Start with guided practice. When a fresh application item is available, it comes before Check Transfer. The guide can use different examples or offer different session lengths based on available material.

Each activity uses words that fit its task. Standard Words are the default in independent practice. In Route 3, retro- starts with retroreflector in Learn. Stretch Words adds examples such as retro-rocket. Printable teaching packs cover selected parts from the same Routes.

When the two formats offer different practice

Independent practice. A word-part card can have example words, a worked lesson, or a sort. Word-based activities offer different subsets of the Route’s parts. For example, Change It uses words with useful changes in form.

Teacher-guided sessions. The activity and session length depend on the examples available for the chosen part. For example, circum-, chron, and pos have a fresh application item in Use It with Standard Words. For put in words such as compute, use Find or Word Hunt to practice spotting the root after explaining its historical meaning.

What Does First Volo Teach?

First Volo teaches students to use meaningful prefixes, roots, and suffixes as tools for understanding and using words. Instruction moves from explicit word-part knowledge and recognition toward structural analysis, meaning inference, word building, and application in context.

What Students Learn

🔎 Recognize Word Parts Identify meaningful prefixes, roots, and suffixes inside increasingly complex words.
💡 Understand Meanings Connect word parts with their meanings and recognize related forms within morpheme families.
🧩 Analyze Word Structure Break words into meaningful parts and explain how those parts contribute to the whole word.
🔍 Infer Word Meaning Use known morphemes as clues to figure out unfamiliar words, with scaffolding when word structure is less transparent.
🏗️ Build & Apply Words Combine meaningful word parts and apply morphologically complex words in sentence context.
🔄 Notice Word Changes Explore how changing a suffix can change a word's form, meaning, or function.

Learning is cumulative. Word parts introduced in earlier Routes continue to appear in later and more complex vocabulary so students can apply previously learned morphology with increasing independence.

Instructional Scope by Migration Route

About the Migration Routes: A morpheme's introduction Route shows when it is first introduced in First Volo. A practice-word Route shows the level at which an individual word is currently used for morphology practice. The grade ranges below provide general instructional guidance rather than fixed student placement. These are not the same as whole-word difficulty. Previously learned word parts continue to appear in later and increasingly complex words.
Activity eligibility varies by task: recognition, segmentation, inference, construction, and contextual-use activities do not necessarily use identical word pools. Educators may choose activities and teacher-led targets according to learner performance and instructional need.
Migration Route 1 — typically Grades 2–325 word-part forms · 27 meanings and functions
25 written word-part entries represent 27 canonical instructional meanings/functions. The two meanings of un- and the adverb- and adjective-forming functions of -ly are separate instructional targets. Both uses of -ly are included in instruction. The four Migration Map sets track progress and rewards for 26 of the 27 targets. Adjective-forming -ly is still pending for map progress and rewards.
