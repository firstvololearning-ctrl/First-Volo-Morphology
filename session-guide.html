STEP 1

🔄 Retrieve

4 min

For this struct session

  • Use 2–3 previously taught word parts. Suggested if already known: re-, pre-, -ion/-tion/-sion.
  • Ask one meaning-recall item, one word-analysis item, and one quick word-meaning item.
  • Do not reteach struct here if this is the student's first encounter with the root.
Sample retrieval prompts
  • What does re- mean?
  • Break apart rebuild. What does each part contribute?
  • What might preheat mean?
  • What does -ion/-tion/-sion often tell you about a word?

Educator does

  • Presents previously taught morphemes and familiar words.
  • Uses brief prompts to revisit meanings, word parts, and word relationships.
  • Allows time for independent retrieval before providing support.

Student does

  • Recalls previously learned morpheme meanings.
  • Identifies familiar word parts and breaks apart or builds familiar words.
  • Explains meanings or relationships when appropriate.

Educator provides support if the student…

  • cannot recall a morpheme meaning → provide two meaning choices.
  • does not notice the relevant word part → highlight or separate it.
  • knows the information but is having difficulty retrieving it → provide a partial verbal or visual cue.
  • is still unable to respond → model one example, then provide a similar item for another attempt.
  • responds successfully with support → reduce the support on the next opportunity.

Suggested word-family teaching

Core idea: struct carries the idea build.

  • construct — locate struct; connect the whole word to building/making.
  • structure — locate struct; discuss how a structure is something built or organized.
  • construction — locate struct; notice the relationship to construct.
  • reconstruct — preserve the fuller analysis re- + con- + struct; do not present it as simply re- + struct.

      STEP 3

      🧩 Apply

      8 min

      For this struct session

      Use the target productively:

      1. Choose or build a word containing struct.
      2. Explain what struct = build contributes to the word.
      3. Use the word in a sentence or explain a sentence containing it.

      Optional frame: “I see struct, which means build. That helps me think this word has to do with ______.”

      Educator does

      • Provides a productive task using the target rather than another recognition-only item.
      • Prompts the student to connect the root to the whole-word meaning.
      • Accepts oral, written, or hands-on demonstration when it shows the intended morphological thinking.

      Student does

      • Builds, selects, or uses a word containing struct.
      • Explains how the root contributes to meaning.
      • Uses the word in a meaningful sentence or context.

      Educator provides support if the student…

      • does not know where to begin → offer two familiar struct words to choose from.
      • can identify struct but cannot explain its contribution → restate struct = build and ask how that idea connects to the whole word.
      • understands the morphology but has difficulty formulating a response → provide the sentence frame.
      • is still unable to complete the task → complete one example together, then provide a parallel item for the student to try.
      • succeeds with support → reduce the support on the next opportunity.

      STEP 4

      🚀 Check Transfer

      6 min

      For this struct session

      Keep these items protected until this step. Do not preteach the transfer word.

      Protected transfer words are no longer exposed on this prototype page.

      Use Program Progress → learner-specific Teacher-Led Session Materials. Check Transfer there selects only unused words from the protected bank tied to the learner's exact taught morpheme.

      Educator does

      • Presents one or more words or contexts that were not directly practiced during the session.
      • Provides minimal support initially and observes whether the student applies known morphology.
      • Separates recognition of the root from successful inference of the whole word when interpreting performance.

      Student does

      • Identifies a familiar morpheme in a new word.
      • Uses word-part knowledge and context to infer as much meaning as possible.
      • Explains what information came from morphology and what information came from context.

      Educator provides support if the student…

      • does not know where to begin → ask, “What part do you recognize?”
      • overlooks struct → highlight or separate the root.
      • recognizes struct but cannot infer the whole-word meaning → direct attention to another meaningful part or the sentence context.
      • still cannot infer the meaning → provide two plausible meaning choices.
      • requires a model → model one item, then immediately provide a different transfer item for another attempt.
      • succeeds with support → reduce the support on the next transfer item.
