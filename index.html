ⓘ What is a Migration Route?

Migration Routes describe the complexity of the word-part content. They are not fixed student grade placements.

Migration Route 1
Typically Grades 2–3
Familiar bases and high-frequency prefixes and suffixes.

Migration Route 2 · Word Families
Typically Grades 4–5
Transparent roots, related academic words, and word families.

Migration Route 3 · Complex Morphology
Typically Grades 6–8
More complex word families, layered morphology, and increasingly independent analysis.

Students can work flexibly across Routes when that better matches their instructional needs. Word Challenge is separate from Migration Route. Standard Words combine Familiar and Academic vocabulary; Stretch Words can be added for additional challenge.

Word Challenge Standard Words are used by default. Add Stretch Words when you want more lexical challenge.
✓ Standard Words Familiar + Academic vocabulary
ⓘ How is word challenge defined?

First Volo keeps three vocabulary classifications in the underlying word inventory: Familiar, Academic, and Stretch.

Standard Words combines Familiar and Academic vocabulary so students have a useful range of accessible and school-relevant words during ordinary practice.

Include Stretch Words adds words classified as Stretch for additional lexical challenge.

Vocabulary classification is separate from Migration Route, which describes the complexity of the morphology being practiced.

First Volo Morphology

What students will learn

Students learn to recognize prefixes, roots, and suffixes, explain what each part adds to a word, and use those clues to figure out unfamiliar words. They also build related words and choose forms that fit a sentence.

End-of-route learning outcomes

By the end of each Migration Route, students will be able to do the following.

  • Route 1 · Typically grades 2–3

    Identify familiar prefixes and suffixes, explain how they change a base word’s meaning or form, and use them to figure out, build, and use related words. For example, use what re- adds to retell to work out repack.

  • Route 2 · Typically grades 4–5

    Break words into meaningful parts, including Greek and Latin roots. Use those parts to explain how words are related, figure out unfamiliar words, and build and use related forms. For example, use port (“carry”) to connect transport and portable.

  • Route 3 · Typically grades 6–8

    Analyze and build words with roots and affixes, and choose related forms that fit a sentence. Infer unfamiliar meanings even when the connection between a part and the whole word is indirect. In Figure It Out, choose a likely meaning, then use a sentence clue to keep or revise it. For example, connect pel (“push” or “drive”) to compel, then use the sentence to tell what someone was made to do.

Ways to teach and practice

All five options draw on the same word parts and Migration Routes.

💻
Independent online learning

Students explore word parts in Learn and practice in Student Mode. Educators can save a lesson or set review.

👩‍🏫
Teacher-guided online sessions

Use the Session Guide and on-screen materials to teach a specific word part, give a cue, and try a fresh example together.

🧩
Printable and hands-on materials

Use teaching packs, word-part cards, and practice pages with one learner or a small group.

🌱
Extension ideas

Use the same word parts in classroom reading, writing, and subject-area vocabulary.

⭐
Word Cards

Revisit unlocked anchor words to help remember what was taught and see how the parts explain the whole word.

How the skills build

1 KNOW THE PARTS

Learn meaningful word parts and connect them with their meanings.

2 NOTICE HOW WORDS WORK

Notice patterns, relationships, and changes across words.

3 REASON WITH THE PARTS

Break apart, build, transform, and compare words to infer meaning.

4 PUT THE PARTS TO WORK

Use morphological knowledge in words and sentence contexts.

5 USE IT SOMEWHERE NEW

Apply learned strategies to unfamiliar words and new contexts.

These five stages show how skills can build toward independent use. Start where the learner needs practice and return to Learn for an example or a refresher.

What the Migration Map tracks

In Student Mode, My Migration Map is where students start practice, see their progress, and play games at unlocked stops. Saved practice earns tokens for sets of word parts. Each token moves Volo one stop closer to Winter Home.

Students can check earlier sets on Routes 2 and 3, or open a set review their educator assigned. A check points students to Learn for parts they miss. Map progress reflects saved practice across activities.

In Word Cards, students can revisit unlocked anchor words to help remember how the parts explain the whole word.

After earning all the tokens for a route, students can try the Migration Challenge to apply what they learned to unfamiliar words before reaching Winter Home.
