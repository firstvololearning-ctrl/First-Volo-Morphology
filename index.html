ⓘ What is a Migration Route?

Migration Routes describe the complexity of the word-part content. They are not fixed student grade placements.

Migration Route 1
Typically Grades 2–3
Familiar bases and high-frequency prefixes and suffixes.

Migration Route 2 · Word Families
Typically Grades 4–5
Transparent roots, related academic words, and word families.

Migration Route 3 · Complex Morphology
Typically Grades 6–8
More complex word families, layered morphology, and increasingly independent analysis.

Students can work flexibly across Routes when that better matches their instructional needs. Word Challenge is separate from Migration Route. Standard Words combine Familiar and Academic vocabulary; Stretch Words can be added for additional challenge.

Word Challenge Standard Words are used by default. Add Stretch Words when you want more lexical challenge.
✓ Standard Words Familiar + Academic vocabulary
ⓘ How is word challenge defined?

First Volo keeps three vocabulary classifications in the underlying word inventory: Familiar, Academic, and Stretch.

Standard Words combines Familiar and Academic vocabulary so students have a useful range of accessible and school-relevant words during ordinary practice.

Include Stretch Words adds words classified as Stretch for additional lexical challenge.

Vocabulary classification is separate from Migration Route, which describes the complexity of the morphology being practiced.

First Volo Morphology

Making Word Structure Visible, Teachable, and Achievable

First Volo Morphology helps students learn meaningful word parts and use them to understand, analyze, build, and use increasingly complex words more independently in reading, writing, and academic language.

How instruction works

💻
Interactive online learning & practice

Engaging activities help students learn, practice, analyze, and apply word structure.

👩‍🏫
Digital and print 1:1 teacher-led sessions

Ready-to-use materials, prompts, teacher language, and support guide focused instruction.

🧩
Printables & hands-on activities

Flexible materials support 1:1 and small-group instruction, practice, and application.

How Morpho Builds Knowledge and Independence

1 KNOW THE PARTS

Learn meaningful word parts and connect them with their meanings.

2 NOTICE HOW WORDS WORK

Notice patterns, relationships, and changes across words.

3 REASON WITH THE PARTS

Break apart, build, transform, and compare words to infer meaning.

4 PUT THE PARTS TO WORK

Use morphological knowledge in words and sentence contexts.

5 USE IT SOMEWHERE NEW

Apply learned strategies to unfamiliar words and new contexts.

Within each set of morphemes, learning is intentionally structured to build from knowledge of meaningful word parts toward increasingly independent analysis, application, and transfer. Learn introduces the morphemes and remains available as a reference throughout the learning process. The five stages describe increasingly complex learning demands; they are not five separate activities or a locked activity checklist.

🧭 More on Teacher-Led Instruction

First Volo is designed to help the educator respond to what is happening during instruction, not simply present a sequence of practice items.

A student begins with an independent attempt. If difficulty occurs, the educator identifies the likely barrier—for example, word-part knowledge, decoding, vocabulary, context, or task directions.

Support is added one step at a time. The student then retries the same demand before moving on.

As performance improves, support is reduced so the student does more of the work independently. Fresh examples can then be used to see whether the student can apply the skill beyond the taught item.

✈️ Migration Routes 1, 2, & 3

First Volo Morphology was developed primarily around Grades 2–8 expectations. Migration Routes describe levels of word-part complexity rather than fixed student placements, and grade labels are not displayed in student-facing activities.

Migration Route 1 Typically Grades 2–3

Practice emphasizes familiar bases and high-frequency prefixes and suffixes.

Migration Route 2 · Word Families Typically Grades 4–5

Practice increasingly includes transparent roots, related academic words, and word families.

Migration Route 3 · Complex Morphology Typically Grades 6–8

Practice includes more complex word families, layered morphology, derivational relationships, and increasingly independent analysis.

Students can work flexibly across Routes when that better matches their instructional needs.

📍 Waypoint Words, Progress & Games

Waypoint Words are instructional anchor words introduced after their component word parts have been introduced through the learning path. Students and educators can revisit them to review meanings, connect new learning with familiar word parts, and support analysis of other words.

The visual migration path reflects progress through each Route. Games unlock at selected points, draw only on word parts available by that point in the learning path, and remain available for replay.

🧩 How Words Are Built
Prefix

Comes before a root or base and adds to or changes its meaning.

Root / Base

Carries the central meaning of the word.

Suffix

Comes after a root or base. It may add meaning, provide grammatical information, or change the role a word plays in a sentence.

Morphology instruction focuses on what each meaningful part contributes and how those meanings work together in the whole word.

For student simplicity, Greek combining forms such as bio, geo, micro, and tele are grouped with roots.

🗂️ Educator Planning Tool

The educator planning tool is organized by learning target and provides connections to grade-level standards, possible IEP objectives, supporting evidence, and instructional considerations.

🍎 Digital + Hands-On Practice

Digital activities and educator-guided materials are designed to work together.

Digital practice supports repeated work with word parts, analysis, inference, word building, and contextual use.

Available printable and hands-on materials support selected parts of the instructional system through word-part cards, word sorts, word-family work, literal and whole-word meaning comparisons, and sentence-context practice. They do not yet represent every digital activity, word part, or learning set.

The printables extend the instructional system rather than functioning as a separate curriculum.

📊 Assessment & Progress Monitoring

First Volo separates instructional practice from assessment and transfer checks.

Pre/Post forms and Check Transfer items use reserved words so students are not simply tested on words they have already practiced during ordinary instruction.

Program Progress can also document completed digital and educator-guided practice.

The goal is to help educators see not only whether a student answered correctly, but also how independently the student is using morphological knowledge over time.

Protect student privacy: Use made-up learner labels and example names. Keep real student names and identifying details out of saved responses.
