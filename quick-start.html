How to use First Volo

Use Pre/Post results to plan practice

Use Pre when you want a baseline and Post to look for growth. Review which items the learner could identify, explain, or apply. Use their responses and any support given to pick a teaching focus.

From the report, select Choose practice from these results. Choose the learner and Route, then select a lesson or open Other practice choices. Select Save learner’s practice plan. The plan saves the practice choices. Assessment reports are saved separately in this browser.

Why First Volo is designed this way

Why there are three Routes
  • Route 1 · Typically grades 2–3. Start with familiar bases, prefixes, and endings, such as play → played and tell → retell. Apel & Diehm (2014), early elementary intervention.
  • Route 2 · Typically grades 4–5. Continue with affixes and introduce more Greek and Latin roots, such as port (“carry”) in portable and transport. Manyak et al. (2018), teaching roots and affixes.
  • Route 3 · Typically grades 6–8. Work with more complex word families, such as pel (“push” or “drive”) in propel and compel. Crosson et al. (2019), Latin roots with adolescent English learners.

Use the learner’s responses to choose a starting Route. Learners in the same grade can show different strengths across word-part tasks. Apel, Henbest, & Petscher (2022).

Use the learner’s actual grade to look up standards. Grades 2–3 use familiar affixes and bases to work out meaning (L.2.4b–c and L.3.4b–c). Grades 4–5 use Greek and Latin roots and affixes as clues (L.4.4b and L.5.4b). Grades 6–8 also verify inferred meanings (L.6.4d, L.7.4d, and L.8.4d). Massachusetts ELA/Literacy Framework · Common Core Language Standards.

How we choose and group word parts
  • Frequency helps prioritize coverage. Give early attention to widely used parts, such as un-, re-, -s/-es, -ed, and -ing. White, Sowell, & Yanagihara (1989).
  • Meaning and function guide grouping. Compare prefixes that mean “not,” endings that do the same grammatical job, or words with the same root. The Migration Map uses separate progress collections for tracking and game access. Manyak, Baumann, & Manyak (2018).
  • Example difficulty depends on the whole word and how clear its parts are. Begin with transparent words such as retell. Later, try examples such as compel, where the meaning connection takes more explanation. Verified Wordsmyth levels are one clue to whole-word difficulty. Apel et al. on base transparency · Wordsmyth Vocabulary Inventory.
Why lessons model, sort, and try a new word
  1. Model. Show what the part contributes in a familiar word. Manyak et al. (2018).
  2. Sort and explain. Ask what the words have in common or what job the ending does. Manyak et al. (2018) · Goodwin (2016).
  3. Try a new word. Check whether the learner can apply the part to an unfamiliar example. Gellert et al. (2021).
  4. Check the sentence in Route 3. Have the learner use a sentence clue to keep or revise their inferred meaning. Graves et al. (2021) · Baumann et al. (2003).
