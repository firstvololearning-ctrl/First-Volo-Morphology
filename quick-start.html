Choosing and teaching word parts across routes

Use the in-app pre/post assessment, recent practice responses, and the learner’s explanations to decide what to teach next. Choose a route for the word parts, a meaning group in Learn to introduce related ideas, and an activity for the skill you want to practice. Educators can practice alongside students or set up independent learning. Migration Map sets group word parts for progress tracking and game access. Educators choose the teaching order based on learner needs. Apel, Henbest, & Petscher (2022) on learner profiles.

How First Volo chooses word parts and examples

  • Frequency and usefulness guide coverage. Start with widely used parts students will meet in many words, such as un- for “not,” re- for “again,” and -s/-es, -ed, and -ing. Frequency is one reason to include a part. Grade connections come from the skills in the standards and the complexity of the examples. White, Sowell, & Yanagihara (1989) on affix frequency.
  • Meaning and function guide grouping. Teach parts with a shared meaning or grammatical job together when that comparison helps. A group can compare different prefixes that mean “not,” or words that share a root, such as portable and transport. The exact groups and route boundaries are First Volo curriculum decisions. Manyak, Baumann, & Manyak (2018), Tables 1–3.
  • Whole-word familiarity and the clarity of the parts guide example difficulty. Begin with words whose parts are easy to spot and explain, such as retell. Add examples that require more reasoning, such as compel, where “drive” helps explain why the word means “force someone to act.” Also consider changes in spelling or pronunciation. Verified Wordsmyth Vocabulary Inventory levels offer an approximate indicator of whole-word difficulty. An entry without a WVI badge has no WVI level in our evidence. Manyak et al. (2018) on clear meaning relationships · Apel et al. on spelling and sound changes · Wordsmyth WVI.

What changes across the routes

  • Route 1 · Typically Grades 2–3. Begin with familiar bases, prefixes, and endings (play → played, tell → retell). Model what the added part does to the familiar word. Apel & Diehm (2014), early elementary intervention.
  • Route 2 · Typically Grades 4–5. Continue with affixes and introduce more Greek and Latin roots (port, “carry,” in portable and transport). Compare how the same root contributes meaning across words. Manyak et al. (2018), grades 4–5 root instruction.
  • Route 3 · Typically Grades 6–8. Extend the same work to more complex word families (pel, “push” or “drive,” in propel and compel). Figure It Out adds an explicit sentence check after the learner chooses a likely meaning. Crosson et al. (2019), Latin roots with adolescent English learners.

The Massachusetts ELA/Literacy Framework and Common Core State Standards connect the routes to grade-level skills. Grades 2–3 use familiar prefixes, affixes, and bases to work out meaning (L.2.4b–c and L.3.4b–c). Grades 4–5 use Greek and Latin roots and affixes as clues (L.4.4b and L.5.4b). Grades 6–8 continue that work and explicitly verify inferred meanings using context or a reference (L.6.4a, b, d, with parallel standards in grades 7 and 8). Use the learner’s actual grade for standards alignment and their current needs to choose a starting route. Massachusetts ELA/Literacy Framework · Common Core Language Standards.

How to teach across routes

The core skills stay the same as words become more complex. Students identify word parts, explain what they mean or do, use them to work out unfamiliar words, and build and use related words. Begin in Learn and choose the next activity based on what the learner can explain.

  1. Meet the part and model it in a word. Invite a prediction from the picture, reveal the meaning, and open a worked example. Say the base or root, point to the added part if there is one, and explain what the parts contribute. The introduction is unscored. Let the learner try the example check. Manyak et al. (2018), explicit modeling and guided practice.
  2. Sort, explain, and build. In Sort It, group words by the target part or meaning. Ask “They all have to do with what?” when the group has a shared meaning. Ask what job the part does when teaching a grammatical ending. Use Build Words to practice combining parts. Manyak et al. (2018), word building and Table 4 word-family grouping · Goodwin (2016), generating related words during reading instruction.
  3. Try the part in a new word. Once the learner explains a familiar example, use the follow-up in Sort It when available or choose Figure It Out. Ask what the known part contributes to the new word. If the learner needs a cue, revisit one familiar example, then try again. Gellert et al. (2021), applying taught parts to untaught words.
  4. Check the sentence in Route 3. In Figure It Out, students use a word part to choose a likely meaning. They then look for a clue in the sentence and keep or revise their answer before submitting. For example, in “The spinning propeller can propel the boat forward through the water,” boat forward helps confirm that propel means “push or drive forward.” Graves et al. (2021), combining word parts and context · Baumann et al. (2003), instruction in morphology and context.

During shared practice, listen for the learner’s reasoning and note the support they needed. In Learn, “I explained it aloud” opens a model for comparison. Educators can listen to that explanation during shared practice. Use both to choose whether to revisit the part or apply it in a new word. Connect word-part practice to reading and writing in class. A review of 22 studies supports integrating morphology with other literacy instruction. Bowers, Kirby, & Deacon (2010) · First Volo research and evidence.

From assessment to a practice plan

After reviewing a completed assessment, choose Choose practice from these results. Select the shared learner, Route, word parts, activity and word challenge. Review these choices and select Save learner’s practice plan. A report’s linked browser learner is preselected only when it matches an eligible shared learner; otherwise choose the learner explicitly.

The plan is an educator decision. Scores do not choose an activity or establish mastery. Use the learner’s responses, support conditions and other evidence. The plan stores practice choices only; it does not upload assessment responses or the Student Code. Saving requires current learner permission, class access and educator ownership. A save is complete only after the confirmation appears.

In the main educator workspace, use Set a practice plan to review, change or clear it. Open practice with this learner opens the saved choices for shared-screen teaching. Adjusting practice choices during a session does not change the saved plan.

In the student workspace, a saved plan appears as Start today’s practice. The learner can use Change practice to access other activities; this does not edit the educator’s plan. A plan remains until the educator changes or clears it. It is not automatically updated each day or after a quiz. The student sees an updated plan when opening or returning to Morphology with an internet connection. This is a starting plan, not a locked sequence or a single-word-part filter.

When you open Morphology

Educator: Choose Work with a learner, Learners & progress, or Educator resources & guide. Start or review an assessment opens the assessment tools for Migration Routes 1, 2, and 3. Assessment results do not automatically assign practice. Review the item results with other evidence, then choose the Route, word parts and activity yourself.

Student: Use the Route and word parts your teacher chose, then choose an activity. Ask your teacher if you are unsure. Standard Words include Familiar and Academic words; Stretch is optional. Activities stay flexible; a tried check is not a mastery score.

Returning: This browser remembers the most recent practice choices separately for each signed-in learner or educator context. Continue opens that activity; it does not resume the exact unanswered question. You can change the choices at any time. These settings are not an assignment and do not sync across devices.

Next Session Guide: In Learners & progress, suggestions use the learner’s latest practice response, not the initial assessment. Review and adjust them using educator judgment.

Assessment records: Formal assessment records are saved in this browser for the educator. Practice cloud sync does not include these assessment records. Keep needed reports separately using the assessment controls.

Word-Part Hoops

Use the Open games button beside practice choices, then choose Shoot hoops beside an unlocked reward. Match the meaning to a word-part ball, adjust aim, then shoot. Line up shot offers aiming help. Harder shots is optional; there is no timer. A correct word part with a missed shot can be retried. Game points do not change learning progress or token unlocks.

The educator start page separates learner selection, word-part choices, and practice activities. Route and Stretch Words are under Adjust practice. Student practice progress appears only in student mode; educators use Learners & progress for records.

A simpler start

In educator mode, open Work with a learner to choose a learner. Learners & progress opens records and management. Begin with one word-part type; Combine word parts, Adjust practice, and Choose another activity expand when needed. Use Open Learn (or the current activity name) to jump into practice.

Educators can use Preview game and Preview hoops without earning learner tokens. Previewing does not change learner progress or unlock student rewards. Students see their earned games.

Choose the next step

Educators first choose Set up independent learning to create or review the practice plan used in student mode, or Work with a learner to open session choices. Learners & progress opens records separately. Practice settings and activities appear after entering the practice path. Next-step guidance names the current learner when selected and the chosen activity, word-part focus, and Route. Back to start returns to the entry choices.

Objectives, standards, and guided planning

From Set up independent learning or Work with a learner, open the existing objective builder. Choose a Migration Route for complexity and the learner’s actual grade separately for CCSS or Massachusetts standards. The builder retains its research and source tabs, offers original example objectives, and links to suitable activities. Some objectives require an educator-led explanation, original sentence, or fresh-word probe beyond the app’s answer choices. Activity links apply session choices; they do not save a plan or award mastery.

In the plan editor, Help me choose a starting point suggests an activity from a teaching need. Use this focus fills the activity only; review every plan field and select Save when ready.

Pull-and-release arcade games

In Hoops, choose the matching word-part ball, then drag the court ball back and down with a mouse or touch and release. The guide previews the flight. Slider, Shoot, and Line up shot remain available. Target Toss uses the same controls to throw toward the matching word-part target. Educators can preview both games; learner reward locks and instructional progress are unchanged.
