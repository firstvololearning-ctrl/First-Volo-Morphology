What Does Research Support?

Morphological instruction explicitly teaches students to notice and use meaningful word parts such as prefixes, roots, bases, and suffixes.

META-ANALYSIS

Goodwin & Ahn, 2013

This meta-analysis synthesized 30 independent studies of morphological instruction with school-age children.

The authors reported a moderate overall positive effect and significant effects for morphological knowledge, phonological awareness, vocabulary, decoding, and spelling.

Overall: d = .32 Morphology: d = .44 Vocabulary: d = .34 Decoding: d = .59 Spelling: d = .30

This meta-analysis did not find significant effects for reading comprehension or fluency. First Volo therefore does not claim that morphology practice alone directly produces gains in every area of reading.

SYSTEMATIC REVIEW

Bowers, Kirby, & Deacon, 2010

This review examined 22 morphological-intervention studies involving students from preschool through Grade 8.

The review found benefits from morphological instruction across literacy outcomes and concluded that it was particularly useful when integrated with other aspects of literacy instruction.

View study ↗
GRADES 5–6

Goodwin, 2016

In a study of 203 fifth- and sixth-grade students, morphological problem-solving was integrated with comprehension-strategy instruction.

Students receiving the morphology component showed stronger outcomes on measures of vocabulary and generation of morphologically related words.

Significant differences were not found for every literacy measure, including reading comprehension and word-reading fluency.

View study ↗
ADOLESCENT ENGLISH LEARNERS

Crosson, McKeown, Moore, & Ye, 2019

This study examined vocabulary instruction with and without explicit analysis of bound Latin roots among 84 English Learner adolescents.

Instruction that included Latin roots produced stronger morphological problem-solving of unfamiliar words, supporting the use of roots as tools for analyzing new academic vocabulary.

Learning of the directly taught academic word meanings was similar across the two instructional conditions, illustrating why morphology is one component of vocabulary instruction rather than a replacement for rich word teaching.

What First Volo takes from this research

First Volo emphasizes explicit teaching of meaningful word parts, repeated analysis across word families, and opportunities to use morphology as a problem-solving strategy. It is intended to complement — not replace — phonics, vocabulary instruction, contextual reading, and other components of comprehensive literacy instruction.

Applying Morphology in Context

First Volo also includes sentence-level application. In Use It, students consider both the meanings of a word's morphemes and the surrounding sentence context when deciding which word fits. This extends practice beyond isolated recognition while keeping morphology as the primary word-analysis strategy.

This design reflects the complementary use of context clues and morphological analysis represented in the Common Core vocabulary standards.

Important distinction

The Common Core standards identify skills students should develop. They do not provide a single official grade-by-grade list assigning every individual prefix, root, suffix, or vocabulary word to a grade.

First Volo Practice Flights are therefore instructional recommendations. They are informed by standards expectations together with morphological complexity, transparency, word accessibility, and curricular sequencing.

How Words and Word Parts Are Selected

Different resources answer different questions. First Volo uses them as complementary evidence rather than treating any one standard, vocabulary list, assessment, or curriculum resource as a complete word-to-grade placement system.

Wordsmyth Vocabulary Inventory (WVI)

The WVI contains 10,000 important vocabulary words organized into five approximate difficulty bands. First Volo uses WVI information as one indicator of vocabulary familiarity and accessibility.

New General Service List (NGSL)

NGSL 1.2 is a corpus-based list of high-utility general English vocabulary. First Volo uses NGSL matches and frequency information as evidence about broad lexical frequency — not as a morphology grade-placement system.

New Academic Word List (NAWL)

NAWL 1.2 is designed to identify important general academic English vocabulary and to work alongside NGSL 1.2. First Volo uses NAWL matches as one indicator that a word occurs in academic language.

Academic Word Finder (Achieve the Core)

The Academic Word Finder helps educators identify high-value Tier 2 vocabulary in text. Its own guidance emphasizes that the tool supports rather than replaces teacher judgment.

First Volo uses it as an additional lens for considering the academic usefulness of vocabulary.

Academic Word Finder ↗

MAP Growth (NWEA)

First Volo uses MAP Growth resources as evidence of instructional relevance and relative difficulty when vocabulary, concepts, or language skills appear within NWEA's assessment framework.

RIT-to-Concept resources and the Learning Continuum provide information about concepts and skills represented within RIT ranges and can help educators interpret the relative difficulty of assessed content. Learning Continuum statements describe content represented in MAP Growth item pools; they do not indicate which items an individual student actually encountered.

The RIT scale itself is grade-independent. NWEA also provides grade-level norms and comparative data that can help place RIT performance in grade context. First Volo therefore considers NWEA evidence alongside other sources rather than treating an individual RIT band as a fixed word-to-grade assignment.

Curriculum Corroboration

Published instructional resources can provide additional evidence that particular morphemes or word families are used in instruction at particular grade levels. First Volo uses resources such as Lexia morphology materials, EL Education's Grades 6–8 affix resources, and grade-specific roots-and-affixes collections as curriculum corroboration.

These sources provide evidence of instructional use. They are not treated as normative lexical grade assignments and do not independently determine a First Volo introduction or practice band.

First Volo Instructional Analysis

External sources are combined with direct analysis of the word itself: its morphemes, segmentation, transparency, structural complexity, whole-word accessibility, academic usefulness, and suitability for a particular activity.

This instructional analysis is a First Volo design decision, not a classification supplied by any external organization.

4

First Volo's Multi-Dimensional Approach

Word difficulty is not treated as a single variable.

Morpheme Introduction

When First Volo recommends introducing a particular prefix, root, combining form, or suffix.

Morphology Practice Band

When analysis of a particular word and its morphological structure is instructionally appropriate.

Word Accessibility

An estimate of when the whole word itself is reasonably accessible to learners.

Vocabulary Level

Familiar, Academic, or Challenge vocabulary, considered separately from morphological complexity.

Transparency

How clearly the visible word parts and their meanings help a learner understand the whole word.

Activity Eligibility

Whether a word is well suited for recognition, decomposition, inference, or independent word building.

Why separate these dimensions?

A familiar word can have opaque internal morphology, while an unfamiliar academic word can contain highly transparent word parts. Separating these dimensions allows First Volo to control both the language demands and the morphological demands of an activity.
